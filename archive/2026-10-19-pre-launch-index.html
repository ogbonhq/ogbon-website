01 / Our thesis

AI compute is priced around the GPU. Most deployed models don’t need a GPU’s worth of compute.

They need memory, privacy and always-on availability.

We build compute for that.

02 / How it works

Memory first. GPUs only where they count.

An Ogbon server pairs terabytes of fast memory with one or two mid-range GPUs.

It works with any model you can deploy: your own, fine-tuned, licensed or open.

03 / Introducing VPU

The runtime that puts every part of a model in the right place.

VPU is Ogbon’s runtime. It is designed to place each part of a model in the right tier, GPU, CPU cache or main memory, and to move activations between them rather than weights.

  1. Placement. Experts live in memory, attention and hot paths on the GPU, cold models on local storage.
  2. Scheduling. Many models share one machine without one customer’s burst slowing another’s.
  3. Efficiency. Fewer bytes read per token, through quantisation, caching and speculative decoding.
  4. Metering. Cost per token for every model and every tenant, so pricing follows usage.

It serves an OpenAI-compatible API, so existing applications work without changes.

04 / Where we win

Utilisation decides what infrastructure costs.

On GPU infrastructure, a large model needs a full eight-GPU node just to fit. At modest traffic, most of it sits idle.

We keep many large models resident in one machine’s memory and share its compute, so the hardware stays busy and customers pay for usage, not idle silicon.

Built for:

  • Very large mixture-of-experts models.
  • Dedicated, single-tenant deployments.
  • Long-tail and fine-tuned models that would sit idle on GPUs.

05 / Deployment

On your premises, or hosted for you.

  • On-premises appliances that run inside your own data centre.
  • Dedicated hosted capacity on the same machines, starting in colocation rather than our own data centre.
  • Single-tenant by design. Your models and your data never share a machine with anyone else.

For model owners and deployers: enterprises with their own models, model companies that need serving capacity, and governments. Enterprise only.

06 / Research, in the open

We measure before we claim.

We began by testing whether ordinary CPUs alone could match a GPU on cost. In our first tests they couldn’t: serving models of up to eight billion parameters on CPUs cost seven to twenty times more per token than a fully loaded NVIDIA H100.

That result shaped what we build. A memory-first design already runs a 235-billion-parameter model on a single server with one mid-range GPU, generating more than ten tokens per second for a single user.

We publish what we measure, including what doesn’t work yet.
